# Engagement Checks

## 1. Identity orientation

Determine whether the visitor can identify the brand/company, primary product/service, and purpose of the website from initial page content. Do not judge writing style.

## 2. AI-to-site context continuity

Ask: If an AI assistant just recommended this brand, can the visitor quickly find the thing they were recommended? Check category links, product/service links, search, breadcrumbs, descriptive navigation, and relevant landing pages.

## 3. Important information visibility

Important information should not require excessive navigation. Examples include product specifications, service descriptions, price, availability, locations, policies, and contact details. Do not require every fact to appear on the homepage.

## 4. Navigation

Inspect whether important pages are connected through normal navigation, including menus, category links, breadcrumbs, internal search, and related products/services.

## 5. Clear next action

Look for contextually relevant actions such as buy, book, contact, demo, compare, learn more, request quote, or sign up. Do not require a CTA on every informational page.

## 6. Content continuity

Compare page titles, headings, navigation labels, and visible content. Link text should not materially mismatch the destination.

## 7. Dead ends

Potential dead ends include important pages with no useful onward navigation, product pages with no related action, service pages without contact or next-step information, or pages that require returning to the homepage to continue.

## 8. Severity

- High: primary user journey is substantially blocked or important information is effectively unreachable
- Medium: meaningful extra navigation or ambiguity is introduced
- Low: minor orientation or next-step improvement
